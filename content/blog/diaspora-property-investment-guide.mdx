Kenyans abroad sent home over USD 4 billion last year, and property remains the single most popular destination for those savings. As the agent who handles most of our diaspora clients, I see the same smart moves and the same avoidable mistakes over and over. Here is the playbook.

## The three structures that work

**Buy to let in the apartment belt.** A two bedroom unit in Kilimani, Kileleshwa or along Kiambu Road, priced between KES 8M and KES 14M, rents reliably to young professionals. Gross yields run 5.5 to 7 percent, and demand is deep enough that voids are short. This is the workhorse of most diaspora portfolios.

**Land in the growth corridors.** Serviced plots in Syokimau, Kitengela, Ruaka and along the bypasses require no management and have historically outpaced inflation. The key is buying inside planned schemes with ready title deeds, not raw land in unplanned areas where boundary disputes live.

**Off plan with staged payments.** Buying a unit during construction at a discount to the completed price, then paying in milestones, lets you enter with a smaller initial outlay. Insist on escrow backed projects and an independent quantity surveyor's progress reports before each release.

## The mistakes that cost the most

The painful stories almost always follow the same three patterns:

1. **Buying without a physical site visit or a trusted representative.** Photos can be years old. We video call every diaspora client live from the property, walking the actual unit, the actual street, at the actual time of day they care about.
2. **Sending money to family to "hold" the purchase.** Too many titles end up in a relative's name. If someone else must transact, use a registered power of attorney, and keep the funds in your control until the transfer registers.
3. **Ignoring ongoing costs.** Service charge, land rates, management fees and vacancy periods eat returns. A rental that looks like 8 percent gross often nets closer to 5.5 percent. Model it honestly before you buy.

## Managing from abroad

Do not self manage from another timezone. A good property manager charges 5 to 8 percent of rent and handles tenant vetting, rent collection, repairs and the annual inspection report. We coordinate management for our clients and send a quarterly statement with photos, so you always know what condition your asset is in.
