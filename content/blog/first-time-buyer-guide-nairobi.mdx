Buying your first home in Nairobi is equal parts exciting and intimidating. The process has real pitfalls, but every one of them is avoidable with preparation. This guide walks through the stages in the order you will actually meet them.

## Stage 1: Know your real budget

Sit down with a mortgage advisor before you fall in love with a listing. Kenyan banks typically finance up to 90 percent of a property's value, but the honest question is what monthly repayment you can sustain alongside transport, school fees and emergencies. A useful ceiling: keep total housing costs under a third of household income.

Remember the transaction costs on top of the price: legal fees at roughly 1.5 percent, stamp duty at 4 percent of the property value, valuation fees and the lender's arrangement fee. On a KES 10M apartment, budget around KES 600,000 for the full closing stack.

## Stage 2: Search with your head, shortlist with your heart

Write down your non negotiables before you start viewing. Commute time, number of bedrooms, security, water supply and parking are the five that first time buyers most often wish they had prioritised. Everything else, finishes, paint colours, the view, can be changed.

View at least six properties before making an offer. The market teaches you what your money buys, and your sixth viewing will make you a far sharper judge than your first.

## Stage 3: The offer and the deposit

Once you find the right property, your agent presents a written offer letter stating price, payment schedule and conditions. If the seller accepts, you pay a booking deposit, commonly 5 to 10 percent, held by the seller's advocate. This takes the property off the market while due diligence runs.

Never pay a deposit directly to a seller's personal account. It must sit with a lawyer.

## Stage 4: Due diligence, the stage that protects you

Your advocate conducts the searches that matter:

- An official search at Ardhi House (the Ministry of Lands registry) to confirm the title is genuine and encumbrance free
- A search at the company registry if the seller is a company, to confirm the signatory has authority
- A rates and rent clearance certificate to confirm no unpaid land rates
- A physical inspection to confirm beacons and boundaries match the survey map

For apartments, also demand the mother title and the lease arrangement. This stage takes two to four weeks and it is where most failed purchases are saved.

## Stage 5: The sale agreement and completion

With searches clean, advocates exchange the sale agreement. Stamp duty is paid to the Kenya Revenue Authority, the transfer is registered at Ardhi House, and the balance of the purchase price moves against the executed transfer. You get keys, and a registered title in your name.

## Stage 6: Move in properly

Transfer the utility accounts into your name, change the locks, service the water pumps if it is a maisonette, and meet the caretaker or residents' association in the first week. Small early courtesies make apartment living smoother for years.
